Math
Launching the Year: Launching the Year
bar graphA graph that uses bars to compare amounts in different categories.
line plotA number line with Xs or dots above it to show how often each value happens.
numerical dataData made of numbers you can measure or count, like heights or test scores.
categorical dataData sorted into groups or categories, like favorite color.
Unit 1: Finding Volume
volumeThe amount of space a solid figure takes up, measured in cubic units.
unit cubeA cube with sides 1 unit long. It has a volume of 1 cubic unit.
cubic unitsUnits for measuring volume, like cubic centimeters (cm³) or cubic inches (in³).
rectangular prismA solid figure with 6 rectangle faces, like a box.
baseIn a prism, the face it rests on. B stands for the area of the base.
heightHow tall a figure is, measured straight up from the base.
lengthHow long a figure is.
widthHow wide a figure is.
layerOne flat level of cubes in a prism. Volume = cubes in one layer × number of layers.
dimensionsThe measurements of a figure: length, width and height.
Mini-Unit: Mini-Unit: Order of Operations
expressionNumbers and operation signs that show a value, with no equal sign. Example: (4 + 2) × 3.
parenthesesThe symbols ( ). Do the work inside them first.
order of operationsThe order to solve an expression: parentheses first, then multiply and divide left to right, then add and subtract left to right.
evaluateFind the value of an expression.
Unit 2: Fractions as Quotients and Fraction Multiplication
fractionA number that names part of a whole or part of a set, like 3/4.
numeratorThe top number of a fraction. It tells how many parts.
denominatorThe bottom number of a fraction. It tells how many equal parts make a whole.
quotientThe answer to a division problem.
productThe answer to a multiplication problem.
area modelA rectangle split into parts to show multiplication.
tape diagramA drawing that looks like a strip of tape, used to show parts and wholes.
Unit 3: Multiplying and Dividing Fractions
unit fractionA fraction with a numerator of 1, like 1/5.
mixed numberA whole number and a fraction together, like 2 1/3.
improper fractionA fraction whose numerator is greater than or equal to its denominator, like 7/3.
areaThe number of square units that cover a flat figure.
scalingMaking something larger or smaller by multiplying. Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes it smaller.
dividendThe number being divided. In 84 ÷ 4, the dividend is 84.
divisorThe number you divide by. In 84 ÷ 4, the divisor is 4.
Unit 4: Wrapping Up Multiplication and Division with Multi-Digit Numbers
standard algorithmThe step-by-step way of multiplying or dividing in columns.
partial productsBreaking a multiplication problem into smaller products and adding them.
partial quotientsDividing in chunks and adding the chunks to find the quotient.
remainderThe amount left over after dividing.
estimateA close answer that is easy to find, often using rounded numbers.
factorA number multiplied to get a product. In 6 × 7 = 42, 6 and 7 are factors.
multipleThe product of a number and a whole number. Multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16 …
Unit 5: Place Value Patterns and Decimal Operations
place valueThe value of a digit because of where it is in a number.
decimalA number with a decimal point, like 3.27, that shows tenths, hundredths and more.
tenthsThe first place to the right of the decimal point. 0.1 = 1/10.
hundredthsThe second place to the right of the decimal point. 0.01 = 1/100.
thousandthsThe third place to the right of the decimal point. 0.001 = 1/1,000.
power of 1010, 100, 1,000 and so on. Multiplying by a power of 10 moves digits to the left.
expanded formA number written as the sum of the value of each digit: 3.27 = 3 + 0.2 + 0.07.
compareDecide which number is greater, less, or if they are equal (>, <, =).
Unit 6: More Decimal and Fraction Operations
common denominatorThe same denominator for two or more fractions.
equivalent fractionsFractions that name the same amount, like 1/2 and 2/4.
unlike denominatorsDenominators that are different, like 1/3 and 1/4.
convertChange a measurement to a different unit, like 3 feet = 36 inches.
metricThe measurement system with meters, liters and grams. Units change by powers of 10.
customaryThe U.S. measurement system with inches, feet, cups, quarts and pounds.
Unit 7: Shapes on the Coordinate Plane
coordinate planeA grid made by a horizontal number line (x-axis) and a vertical number line (y-axis).
originThe point (0, 0), where the x-axis and y-axis meet.
x-axisThe horizontal number line on the coordinate plane.
y-axisThe vertical number line on the coordinate plane.
ordered pairTwo numbers (x, y) that name a point. Go over x, then up y.
quadrilateralA polygon with 4 sides and 4 angles.
parallelogramA quadrilateral with 2 pairs of parallel sides.
rhombusA parallelogram with 4 equal sides.
trapezoidA quadrilateral with at least 1 pair of parallel sides.
hierarchyA diagram that shows how groups fit inside other groups. Every square is a rectangle.
Mini-Unit: Mini-Unit: Measurement
convertChange a measurement to a different unit, like 3 feet = 36 inches.
metricThe measurement system with meters, liters and grams. Units change by powers of 10.
customaryThe U.S. measurement system with inches, feet, cups, quarts and pounds.
meterA metric unit of length. 1 meter = 100 centimeters.
literA metric unit of liquid volume. 1 liter = 1,000 milliliters.
gramA metric unit of mass. 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams.
footA customary unit of length. 1 foot = 12 inches.
poundA customary unit of weight. 1 pound = 16 ounces.
quartA customary unit of liquid volume. 1 quart = 2 pints = 4 cups.
Science
Unit 0: Building a Community of Scientists
observeUse your senses to notice details.
evidenceFacts or data that support an idea.
investigateCarefully study something to answer a question.
modelA drawing, object or diagram that shows how something works.
Unit 1: Fueling Athletes
cellThe smallest unit of a living thing.
tissueA group of similar cells working together.
organA body part made of tissues that does a job, like the heart.
organ systemA group of organs working together, like the digestive system.
organismA living thing.
multicellularMade of many cells. Humans are multicellular.
functionThe job something does.
heartThe muscle that pumps blood through the body (circulatory system).
blood vesselsTubes that carry blood: arteries, veins and capillaries.
lungsOrgans that take in oxygen and get rid of carbon dioxide (respiratory system).
tracheaThe windpipe that carries air to the lungs.
brainThe organ that controls the body (nervous system).
spinal cordA bundle of nerves in the backbone that carries messages to and from the brain.
nervesFibers that carry messages between the brain and the body.
esophagusThe tube that carries food from the mouth to the stomach.
stomachThe organ that mixes and breaks down food with juices.
intestinesLong tubes where nutrients are absorbed and waste moves out (digestive system).
bonesHard parts of the skeletal system that support and protect the body.
musclesBody parts that pull on bones and organs to make movement.
oxygenA gas in the air that cells need to use energy from food.
carbon dioxideA waste gas the body breathes out.
nutrientsParts of food the body uses for energy and growth.
voluntary musclesMuscles you control, like the ones in your arms and legs.
involuntary musclesMuscles that work on their own, like the heart.
wasteWhat the body does not need and gets rid of.
Unit 2: Paws, Patterns and Pedigrees
instinctA behavior an animal is born knowing how to do, like a spider spinning a web.
learned behaviorA behavior an animal learns from experience or from others, like a dog learning to sit.
inherited traitA characteristic passed from parents to offspring, like eye color.
acquired traitA characteristic gained during life from the environment, like a scar or knowing how to read.
offspringThe young of a living thing.
parentA living thing that has produced offspring.
generationAll the offspring at the same step in a family line.
geneticsThe study of how traits are passed from parents to offspring.
characteristicA feature or quality of a living thing.
Unit 3: The Gray Ghosts
ecosystemAll the living and nonliving things in an area and how they interact.
estuaryWhere a river meets the ocean and fresh water mixes with salt water.
salt marshA coastal wetland flooded by salt water at high tide.
populationAll the organisms of one kind living in the same area.
communityAll the populations living together in an area.
habitatThe place where an organism lives and gets what it needs.
terrestrialLiving on land.
aquaticLiving in water.
bioticLiving parts of an ecosystem, like plants and animals.
abioticNonliving parts of an ecosystem, like water, soil and sunlight.
producerAn organism that makes its own food from sunlight, like a plant.
consumerAn organism that eats other organisms for energy.
decomposerAn organism that breaks down dead things and returns nutrients to the soil, like fungi.
food chainA path that shows how energy passes from one organism to the next.
food webSeveral food chains connected together.
energy transferEnergy moving from one organism to another when one eats the other.
stableStaying about the same over time.
Unit 4: The Great Smoky Mountains
weatherWhat the air is like at a certain place and time.
climateThe usual weather of a place over many years.
precipitationWater that falls from clouds: rain, snow, sleet or hail.
temperatureHow hot or cold something is, measured with a thermometer.
air pressureThe weight of the air pressing down. Falling pressure often means stormy weather.
wind speedHow fast the air is moving, measured with an anemometer.
wind directionThe direction the wind comes from, shown by a wind vane.
barometerA tool that measures air pressure.
thermometerA tool that measures temperature.
anemometerA tool that measures wind speed.
wind vaneA tool that shows wind direction.
rain gaugeA tool that measures how much rain fell.
air massA huge body of air with about the same temperature and moisture.
frontThe boundary where two air masses meet. Fronts often bring weather changes.
forecastA prediction of what the weather will be.
hurricaneA huge storm that forms over warm ocean water with very strong winds.
tornadoA spinning column of air that reaches from a storm cloud to the ground.
Gulf StreamA warm ocean current that flows up the East Coast past North Carolina.
sea breezeA cool wind that blows from the sea to the land during the day.
land breezeA wind that blows from the land to the sea at night.
evaporationWhen liquid water heats up and changes into water vapor.
transpirationWhen plants give off water vapor through their leaves.
condensationWhen water vapor cools and changes into liquid drops, like clouds or dew.
water vaporWater in the form of a gas.
water cycleThe path water takes as it moves between Earth's surface and the air, powered by the sun.
Unit 5: The Green Lady and Where Did It Come From? Where Did It Go?
substanceA kind of matter, like water or salt.
propertySomething you can observe or measure about matter, like color, mass or texture.
interactionWhen two or more things act on or affect each other.
qualitative dataObservations described with words, like “the liquid turned green.”
quantitative dataObservations with numbers, like “the mass was 52 grams.”
massThe amount of matter in an object.
weightHow strongly gravity pulls on an object.
dissolveWhen a solid mixes completely into a liquid, like sugar in water.
physical changeA change in size, shape or state that does not make a new substance.
chemical changeA change that makes a new substance, like rust or a burning match.
conservation of massIn a closed container, the mass before and after an interaction stays the same.
Unit 6: An EGGcellent Material
heatingAdding thermal energy to make something warmer.
coolingTaking away thermal energy to make something colder.
meltingWhen a solid changes into a liquid because it is heated.
freezingWhen a liquid changes into a solid because it is cooled.
reversible changeA change that can be undone, like melting ice and freezing it again.
irreversible changeA change that cannot be undone, like cooking an egg.
materialWhat something is made of, like wood, metal or plastic.
Unit 7: Skateology
forceA push or a pull.
motionA change in an object's position.
gravityThe force that pulls objects toward Earth.
frictionA force that slows motion when two surfaces rub together.
massThe amount of matter in an object.
speedHow fast an object moves: distance ÷ time.
directionThe way an object moves: north, south, east, west, left, right, forward or back.
positionWhere an object is compared to something else.
distanceHow far an object moves.
non-contact forceA force that acts without touching, like gravity or magnetism.
